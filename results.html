From sun to heat

The UTCI is how hot the weather feels, in °C. On June 24 at 15:00 a typical sunlit sidewalk felt like 45.9 °C, at the edge of extreme heat stress (46 °C); a step into shade took off about 5 °C.

  • Tree shade is as cool as building shade. The shade cost counts it as half as good; in the heat model it takes off 112%–115% of the step from sun to building shade at 15:00 (94% at 8:00). Shaded streets still receive heat from walls and pavement that stood in the sun; a tree also shades the ground under it.
  • Shade does not make a walk comfortable. Building shade stays at very strong heat stress from 10:00 to 17:00 on June 24.

UTCI = mean along the walk, change against the shortest. Extreme heat = metres with UTCI ≥ 46 °C, and the reduction for walks that have any.

  • The two walks mostly agree: for 71%–77% of walks of 300–800 m they are the same. Where they differ, the coolest walk takes the tree-lined street.
  • A little cooler for a little more distance: about 0.1 °C cooler than the shadiest walk and 1.4–2.2 times the cut in extreme heat, for detours of 0.3%–0.9%.
  • Rapid-transit walks gain most: −0.72 °C against −0.54 °C from bus stops.
  • Averages hide the walks with no choice. The gain is largest in the morning (−0.82 °C at 09:00), small around midday (−0.33 °C at 14:00) and again in the evening (−0.13 °C at 19:00), when the low sun no longer heats the sunny side much.

Why the coolest walk is cooler

At the same detour the UTCI cost cools walks 12%–14% more than the shade cost. Counting tree shade as building shade adds only about 1%: most of the gain comes from differences within each shade class, such as sun next to hot walls against sun on a lawn.

Robustness. With 5% or 15% of the sun passing through tree crowns instead of 3%, tree shade’s share of the building-shade relief at 15:00 moves from 112% to 110% and 99%, still far from the half assumed by the shade cost. See the technical notes.

Opening the mapped buildings puts about one in five shadiest walks indoors and removes another 1.9–2.3 points of sun, without longer walks. The Indoor Contribution Index stays near zero: passages here are an equally long, cooler option, not a shortcut, unlike the malls and linked towers of Wen et al.’s Dubai stations. Indoor routes are taken mainly when they are on the way; walks of 800–1,200 m use them 2.4–3.0 times as often as walks of 300–800 m.

Limitations

  • Campus and park paths are missing outside University City, so cooler options there are understated.
  • “No cooler option” mixes two cases: no cooler street, or one not worth the detour.
  • Two hot, sunny days in 2025; trees from leaf-off LiDAR and 2018 land cover; buildings from 2022.
  • One weather cell: no neighbourhood differences in air temperature or wind.
  • Trees let a fixed 3% of the sun through.
  • Indoor passages assume open, air-conditioned buildings; they have no UTCI.
